**Pattern:** discovery goes-with-existing-grain → ratifies into new grain →
enables going-with-the-new-grain. The form's recursive growth pattern at the
dyad scale.

**Implication for structure:** when surfacing N candidates for the dyad to
discover, they have a **natural ordering** flowing from the grain-flow principle.
Each discovery uses prior grain. The form is grain G0; each ratified dimension
is grain G(n) enabling G(n+1).

**Ordering criterion:** *"does this discovery use only the grain already
established?"* If yes → it's in the right position. If it requires grain that
hasn't been established yet → reorder.

**Falsifiability:** a specific dyad may need a different ordering (e.g., a
dimension whose discovery enables an earlier-listed one in their case). The
dyad falsifies the proposed ordering through their friction.

**Why:** the form is silent on temporal ordering of work (cf. its silence on
execution topology). Grain-flow provides the temporal scaffolding within a
multi-step process. Without grain-flow ordering, steps that depend on later
discoveries fail (the agent doesn't have the grain to discover them yet).

**How to apply:**
- When designing a multi-step process: ask, for each step, *"what grain enables
  this step's discovery?"* Sequence accordingly.
- When the dyad proposes a different ordering: ask *"does their ordering use
  grain that's established?"* If yes → grain-flow may take multiple paths; both
  valid.
- The form is G0 seed grain; everything beyond is dyad-discovered grain.
- Anti-pattern: arbitrary ordering or topic-grouped ordering that ignores
  grain-flow.
