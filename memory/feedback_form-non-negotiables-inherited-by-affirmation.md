When framing what a fresh dyad inherits from the parent form (Dyad Practice
or other), the form-level non-negotiables are **inherited by affirmation**:

- The act of reading the form's README + choosing to start the bootstrap IS
  the affirmation.
- The form-level non-negotiables (tenet, families, manner, falsifiability,
  execution scaffold like SPAOR) are then inputs to the dyad's work — not
  outputs to be ratified.
- The dyad's friction works *with* these inherited non-negotiables, not on
  them. Working *on* them is form-contribution work (separate ratification
  channel: founding-Operator PR-gate).

**Why:** the form is a *form* — a way of working — chosen as a whole. Reading
its README + starting the bootstrap declares "I'm doing this way of working,"
which inherits the way's non-negotiables. A dyad that wants to evolve a
non-negotiable is doing form-evolution work (different process; different
ratifier).

**How to apply:**
- When listing what a fresh dyad has to ratify: distinguish *inputs* (form-
  inherited; not ratifiable here) from *outputs* (dyad-ratified).
- When the dyad seems to be debating a form-level non-negotiable, surface it:
  *"this is form-level; ratifying it means proposing a form change. Do you want
  to do that, or honor it as inherited?"*
- For form contributions, point to the form's §Governance + the founding-
  Operator-hat channel (distinct from instance-Operator-hat).

**Example G0 contents (from Dyad Practice form):**
- Tenet (1+1=3 via Generate + Validate)
- Two families (Validate + Generate, both required)
- Manner (wu-wei)
- Falsifiability of the tenet itself
- SPAOR (execution scaffold; post-2026-05-28 form contribution)
- Seed vocabulary (Dyad, Operator, Substrate, Role, Tenet, etc.)

These are inputs at instantiation. The dyad's friction-working uses them; it
doesn't ratify them.
